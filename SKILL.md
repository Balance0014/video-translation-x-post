# X 视频译制与发帖 / X Video Localization & Posting

Aim for a localized edition with an original feel through natural Chinese writing, deliberate subtitle placement, an accurate opening hook, considered pacing, and clear attribution. Deliver the requested video, editable Chinese `.srt`, a concise factual summary, and an X post when requested. Follow the user's latest instructions over any saved defaults. Never imply that cosmetic edits make a reused video original under X's rewards rules.

## Start each job

1. Run `python3 scripts/state.py init`; read the returned preferences and recent ledger entries. State lives outside this shareable skill and is optional for first-time users.
2. Identify the user's input file, output location, watermark, and requested publishing assets. Inspect duration, resolution, audio, subtitle tracks, and representative opening, middle, and ending frames. Confirm whether English captions are burned into the image, available as a subtitle track, or absent. Do not infer this from metadata alone.
   Treat the entire user-provided video as the edit scope unless the user explicitly requests a cut or excerpt. A long runtime or intent to post on X does not authorize shortening it. If an optional scope question goes unanswered, continue with the full file.
3. Reuse trustworthy existing transcripts or subtitles. Otherwise transcribe with an available ASR tool; `scripts/transcribe.py` is one local option. Check names, numbers, claims, and cut-off endings against audio and frames before translating.
   For long videos, work in time-coded batches and save the transcript, translations, and corrections on disk. Reuse checked text instead of retranscribing or sending the entire transcript through the model again after each correction.

## Translate and edit

- Write idiomatic Chinese that preserves meaning, uncertainty, numbers, and speaker attribution. Keep cues readable and synchronized; save the editable `.srt`.
- Before a long full render, read and polish the entire Chinese SRT for native phrasing, meaning, terminology, and completeness. Check uncertain lines, names, numbers, speaker changes, and shot or slide transitions against the source. Check every cue for empty text, invalid timing, and overlap, and compare subtitle coverage with the source's beginning and ending. Correct the SRT before rendering; spot checks alone do not establish native-level translation quality.
- If English captions exist, keep them readable and place Chinese below them. If they are absent, place Chinese directly on the video. Avoid a large solid black subtitle bar. Use a restrained broadcast-style font, outline, and background treatment; do not cover faces, demonstrations, charts, or logos.
- Add only the requested watermark. Every X-ready export must include a brief, accurate on-screen opening hook that points to the video's core claim; do not treat this as optional or omit it because the spoken opening is already clear. Check the first seconds of the rendered sample and final file to confirm the hook is present without covering a speaker, existing captions, logos, or the watermark. Mirror only after checking that no visible text, logos, charts, or captions would become reversed. Do not use mirroring, color changes, frame dropping, or MD5 changes as a claim of originality.
- Do not add inaudible audio or invisible visual adversarial perturbations as a routine way to evade content matching. Such attacks are model-specific, may disappear during upload transcoding, and do not establish originality. Keep the audio and image clean; describe the result as a localized edition unless it meets the platform's substantive originality criteria.
- Use 1.2× speed only when requested or saved as the user's preference. At a fixed output frame rate, speeding up naturally samples fewer video frames; avoid extra frame removal that causes jitter. Review speech, timing, logic, and scene transitions after speeding up. At the final QA gate, compare every cue in the retimed SRT with the source-timeline Chinese SRT using `scripts/verify.py --source-subtitles`; also play representative opening, middle, transition, and ending moments to confirm the spoken words, subtitle, and visible action or slide agree. A duration check or mathematically retimed SRT alone does not prove audio/visual synchronization.
- Render a short sample first. Inspect it and settle the subtitles, hook, layout, and speed before starting the full render; avoid a costly rerender caused by known draft errors. Fix and rerender if final QA finds a real defect. After any speed change, deliver the retimed editable SRT matching the final video's timeline. Run `scripts/verify.py`, inspect opening/middle/end frames and audio, and confirm the delivered copy matches the verified file.

## Publish package and memory

- For an interview clip's X post, first give a new reader real context: name the program and briefly say what kind of show it is (and its host when useful); name the guest and a verified role or credential that explains why their view matters. State the episode topic when it fits, then move to the concrete hook and what viewers learn. Merely listing the show and guest names is insufficient. After the hook, explain the speaker's argument with enough concrete detail to stand on its own: include the key reasoning, examples, or stakes supported by the clip. When the material warrants it, the main copy may be roughly twice as long as a terse draft; do not pad it to meet a length target. If the intended X post format cannot hold the useful detail, use a concise main post followed by ordered replies rather than dropping it. For other videos, open with the concrete surprising point. Attribute claims as the speaker's prediction or opinion, verify factual marketing claims, and identify the source when known. Do not publish or message anyone without user authorization.
- Deliver at the user's requested location, including the video and editable subtitles; provide the X text if requested. Record the actual input, choices, output paths, checks, and lesson learned in the local ledger. Update preferences only when the user establishes a reusable preference.
